## Spend less, build less

Sustainability is often presented as something to be bought: a certified product, a smart system, a green wall. Many of these have value, but for an organisation with a modest budget they can obscure a simpler truth. The material that is never purchased has no manufacturing footprint, no transport, no installation waste and no disposal cost. A frugal fit-out is already a lower-impact one.

This article is about choices that cost little or nothing. Most of them are decisions rather than products, and many continue to pay back every month the office is in use.

## Keep what already works

Before planning any demolition, walk the space with someone who can judge condition honestly. Many offices in Indian cities change hands with useful fit-out left behind: a ceiling grid, light fittings, flooring, partitions, toilets, a pantry counter. Each item kept is a cost avoided and waste prevented.

Ask simple questions of each element. Is it structurally sound? Does it meet current fire and safety requirements? Can it be cleaned, repainted or repaired? Does it fit the new layout with minor changes? A ceiling grid with stained tiles may need only new tiles. A tired partition may need only a new coat of paint. A pantry counter may need new shutters rather than a new carcass.

Sometimes the layout can bend to fit what exists. Placing a meeting room where an old cabin already stands, rather than demolishing it and rebuilding elsewhere, saves materials, money and time.

## Set the AC sensibly

Air conditioning is usually the largest energy cost in an Indian office, and the most effective step is free: set a sensible temperature. The Bureau of Energy Efficiency has made 24°C the [default setting for star-labelled room air conditioners](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1598508), and its guidance to commercial establishments encourages settings in that comfort range rather than the colder temperatures many offices run at.

A few habits support this:

- Agree a standard set point and label the remotes or controllers accordingly.
- Switch off AC in meeting rooms and cabins when they are empty.
- Close blinds on sun-facing windows during the hottest hours to reduce heat gain.
- Keep doors to air-conditioned areas closed and check that windows seal properly.
- Clean filters regularly; clogged filters force units to work harder, particularly during dusty months.

Ceiling or pedestal fans used together with AC can make a slightly higher set point feel comfortable.

## Switch to LED and add simple controls

If the office still has fluorescent tubes or older fittings, replacing them with LED fittings or retrofit lamps is one of the simplest upgrades available. LEDs use less energy, produce less heat and last longer.

Basic controls add further savings without a complex system:

- Occupancy sensors in toilets, stores, meeting rooms and cabins.
- Separate switches for the rows of fittings nearest the windows, so they can be turned off on bright days.
- A clear end-of-day routine for whoever leaves last.

## Furnish without buying new

New furniture is often a large share of a fit-out budget, and it carries a substantial material footprint. Alternatives are worth considering:

- Reuse furniture from your previous office, even if it is mixed. A consistent colour of paint or upholstery can tie disparate pieces together.
- Buy refurbished workstations and chairs. Several Indian suppliers deal in used office furniture, and a good chair refurbished often outperforms a cheap new one.
- Rent furniture for teams whose size is uncertain.
- Choose simple, repairable designs over complex ones with proprietary parts.

### Use less material overall

Every surface that is clad, panelled or decorated adds material and cost. A restrained interior, with painted walls, a few well-chosen finishes and exposed elements where appropriate, uses less of both. Avoid decorative layers that serve no function. Specify standard sizes of boards and tiles to reduce offcuts. Choose low-VOC paint for the surfaces you do treat; it is now widely available at ordinary prices.

Plants are an inexpensive way to soften a space. Choose hardy species suited to indoor conditions and local climate, and plan who will water and care for them.

## Run it with care

### Organise waste where it is made

Most office waste is generated in the pantry and around the printer. Provide clearly labelled bins for wet waste, dry recyclables and paper, sized for the amount actually produced. Many Indian cities require waste segregation at source, and local municipal guidance sets out the categories. Arrange collection of electronic waste through an authorised recycler.

Reduce the waste stream as well: use washable cups and plates in the pantry, buy supplies in bulk, set printers to double-sided by default and encourage digital documents.

### Maintain as a habit

An office that is well maintained uses less energy and lasts longer. Schedule regular cleaning of AC filters and coils, check for dripping taps and running cisterns, replace failed lamps promptly and repair small damage before it becomes large. Read the electricity bill monthly and look for unexpected changes. These routines cost little and prevent waste quietly.

## Start with three decisions

If the list feels long, begin with three: keep whatever is sound, set the AC to a sensible temperature, and buy less. These choices alone shape an office that is lighter on resources and on the budget. Test each new purchase against a simple question: can something we already have do this job?
