## Why a single rate misleads

The first question most people ask about an office interior is the rate per square foot. It is a reasonable question and an unhelpful answer. A rate divides a total by an area, and so it carries every assumption behind that total without showing any of them. Two quotations at the same rate can describe entirely different offices: one with new air conditioning, a full fire system and ergonomic chairs, the other with paint, loose furniture and the landlord's existing ceiling left in place.

The useful approach is to work in the other direction. Understand what drives cost in your particular building, decide which of those drivers you can influence, and only then look at totals. In Bengaluru, a handful of factors account for most of the difference between one project and the next.

## The condition of the space at handover

Nothing moves the budget more than what the landlord hands over. A bare shell in a new tower may arrive as a concrete slab, a core with toilets and lifts, and a single electrical supply point at the riser. Everything inside the boundary is yours to provide: flooring, ceiling, lighting, air conditioning, fire detection and sprinklers below the ceiling, power distribution and data.

A warm shell changes the picture. It may already include a grid ceiling, base lighting, an air-conditioning system to a fixed layout and sprinklers at a standard spacing. That sounds like a saving, and often it is, but only if the existing layout suits your plan. Enclosed rooms placed under a ceiling designed for open floor will need their own supply and return air, extra sprinkler heads and new light positions. The more your plan departs from the base build, the more of that base build is altered or removed.

A previously occupied floor sits somewhere between the two. Reusing what is sound can be economical; stripping out what is not adds a demolition and disposal cost that is easy to forget. Ask for the handover condition in writing, with drawings, before anyone prices anything.

## The building and its rules

Many Bengaluru offices sit in managed tech parks or Grade A towers, and these come with a fit-out manual. It specifies what you may and may not do: approved contractors or minimum insurance cover, materials permitted in common areas, how services connect to the building systems, and the documents required before work begins. Some parks require fire and electrical drawings to be reviewed by their own consultants. Each of these steps takes time and some carry fees.

Working hours matter as much. Where noisy work, deliveries or hot work are limited to nights and weekends, labour costs rise and progress slows. Material movement may be restricted to service lifts at fixed times. None of this is unreasonable in an occupied building, but it should be read before the programme is set, not discovered once the contractor is on site.

## The services behind the ceiling

Mechanical, electrical and fire services are where budgets most often go wrong, because they are invisible in a rendering. Questions worth answering early:

- **Air conditioning.** Is the building on a central chilled-water plant, or will you install your own VRV or split units? Who supplies the outdoor units, where do they sit, and is there space and structural capacity for them?
- **Fire.** Does the existing sprinkler and detection layout cover your proposed rooms, or will heads and detectors need to be added and the system re-tested? Changes generally need to align with the [National Building Code of India 2016](https://www.bis.gov.in/standards/technical-department/national-building-code/) and the local fire authority's requirements.
- **Power and backup.** How much load has the landlord allocated, and how much of it is on DG backup? Server rooms, kitchens and dense workstation areas may need a UPS, and a UPS needs a room, ventilation and battery space.
- **Ceiling height.** A generous slab-to-slab height allows ducts, cable trays and sprinkler pipes to run without conflict. A tight one forces coordination, bulkheads or an exposed ceiling, each with its own cost.

## Furniture and the materials palette

Furniture can be a large share of the total, and it is the part most open to choice. A workstation can be a fabricated desk built on site or a system product bought from a manufacturer. A chair can be a basic task chair or a fully adjustable one. The spread between options is wide, and the decision belongs with the people who will use them.

Materials follow the same logic. Imported stone, timber, acoustic panels or specialist fittings can carry long lead times, currency exposure and import duties. Many equivalent products are made in India and can be inspected before they are ordered. The question is not whether imported is better, but whether a particular imported item does something the local alternative cannot.

## The ambition of the brief

The final driver is the brief itself. A floor of open desks, a few meeting rooms and a pantry is a different undertaking from a floor with a client experience centre, a training room with retractable partitions, a studio for recording and a terrace. Each additional room type brings walls, doors, acoustics, services and furniture with it.

Ambition is not a problem to be removed. It needs to be named. When the brief lists what must be present, what would be good to have and what can wait, the cost conversation becomes a set of choices rather than a single number to be argued down.

## How to compare quotations honestly

When proposals arrive, set the totals aside for a moment and check the scope. Confirm that each one assumes the same handover condition, the same area measured the same way and the same services scope. Look for what is excluded: landlord approvals, MEP design, furniture, signage, IT cabling, loose items and GST are common omissions. A low figure with long exclusions is not a low figure.

Ask each bidder to state their assumptions about working hours, approvals and lead times. Keep a modest contingency, held by you and spent only on recorded changes or genuine unknowns. Read the building and its fit-out manual before any figure is fixed. Understand the drivers first, and the cost will make sense when it arrives.
