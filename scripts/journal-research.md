# Journal research: competitor topic map (office interiors, India)

**Purpose.** Plan an original journal for an Indian office design-and-build studio. The map shows which topics Indian and Asian workplace design-and-build firms already publish on, what readers are really asking, where those posts fall short, and the distinct angle we can take.

**Date researched:** 2026-10-26

**Rule.** We read competitor posts only to learn their topics and angles. We record titles and URLs and nothing else: no sentences, headings, statistics, quotes, images or structure. Every journal article must be written from scratch, from general professional reasoning and the public primary sources listed at the end. We have no project history to cite, so articles never claim one. If a fact can only be found in a competitor post, leave it out.

Explain efficiency ratios as a method, not a promise.

## Cross-cutting observations

- **Repetition is the main weakness.** OfficeBanao publishes 5–10 near-duplicate posts on reception, colour and ceilings, and templated city pages for cost, "top 10 designers" and trends. One deep, well-sourced article per topic will stand out.
- **Missing everywhere:** stated cost assumptions, landlord handover conditions, NBC 2016 fire and egress, IS 3646 lighting levels, measured acoustics, monsoon and humidity effects, power backup, and SEZ/STPI or tech-park approvals.
- **Flipspaces** is closest to the practical, India-specific positioning. Differentiate through depth (worked examples, checklists, primary sources) rather than claims.
- **Space Matrix and Gensler** own enterprise/GCC thought leadership. Do not compete on trend essays. Compete on how-to depth for 20–300 seat offices.
- **Tétris** has no live India/APAC content. Its EMEA cost-guide format (dated, annual, with stated assumptions) is a useful model for a yearly "India fit-out cost drivers" note built on stated, dated assumptions and public sources.
